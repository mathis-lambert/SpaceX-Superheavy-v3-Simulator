# Visual reference direction

The six supplied images are preserved, with hashes, under `ArtSource/References/UserSupplied`. They are reference material, not licensed runtime textures. Original photographers/licenses and vehicle generations have not been verified. The observations below are art-direction goals, not dimensions or physical measurements extracted from photographs.

## Grounded lighting and industrial scale

![Warm horizon and restrained steel highlights](../../ArtSource/References/UserSupplied/reference-1.png)

Use a low, broad warm horizon with cooler overhead illumination. Steel should retain broad reflections and fine roughness variation; small hard specular highlights should not turn the complete vehicle into a white silhouette. Tower trusses, platforms, pipes and mount details need a consistent human scale.

![Starbase skyline, wet ground and layered clouds](../../ArtSource/References/UserSupplied/reference-2.png)

Separate foreground drainage and wet asphalt, service infrastructure, towers and distant cloud layers. Add detail around believable maintenance routes and equipment clusters rather than uniformly scattering clutter. Preserve readable silhouettes at spectator distances.

![Dusk atmosphere and selective site illumination](../../ArtSource/References/UserSupplied/reference-3.png)

Let warm direct light coexist with cooler shadow fill. Night lighting belongs to floodlights, access platforms, obstruction beacons and active equipment. Broad luminous fog and uniformly bright asphalt should not replace local light sources.

## Space, plumes and catch

![Curved Earth, dark space and exposed vehicle hardware](../../ArtSource/References/UserSupplied/reference-4.png)

Preserve the thin atmospheric limb and deep background. Earth detail should vary with footprint and altitude; local orthophotos cannot substitute for resolved cloud fields and large-scale geographic imagery from high orbit. Exposure, reflected blue light and separation scale must be evaluated together.

![Landing plume, changing attitude and tower clearance](../../ArtSource/References/UserSupplied/reference-5.png)

The plume has an intense compact core and a softer turbulent boundary. Vehicle attitude, camera tracking and atmospheric shimmer contribute motion; decorative camera effects must never move the physical body. This photograph is a visual reference, not proof that this exact approach is safe for the project's catch geometry.

![Catch, broad ground condensation and readable flight graphics](../../ArtSource/References/UserSupplied/reference-6.png)

Ground vapor spreads and catches local light while portions of the structure remain visible. Match density scale, light falloff and exposure together. The interface direction is restrained monochrome telemetry, deliberate spacing and clear hierarchy; do not reproduce a brand wordmark.

## Public research references

- [SpaceX engine-cluster photograph, October 2025](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/1976058665280078003): study concentric engine placement, nozzle interiors and aft mechanical density. Identify the generation before taking dimensions from it.
- [SpaceX vehicle updates](https://new.spacex.com/updates): track generation changes before combining earlier catch footage with V3 labels and geometry.
- [NASA launch plume/water simulation](https://www.nasa.gov/aeronautics/artemis-sls-launch-sim/): useful multiphase modelling reference; the SLS launch environment is not Starbase.
- [USGS NAIP image service and attribution](https://imagery.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/USGSNAIPImagery/ImageServer?f=pjson): public orthophoto source. The new 4,000-pixel tiles cover 3 km each, approximately 0.75 m per output pixel. Output spacing does not prove the native resolution or date of every source image in the mosaic.
